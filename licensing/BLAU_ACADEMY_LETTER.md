## The four framings — pick one to lead with

### Variant 1 — Forthright fair-use (seeking your blessing)

> I want to be transparent about how your dictionary has helped this project, and to make sure I am on the right footing with you. The public site uses only a small set of original notes that *cite* Blau — never his article text — each carrying an explicit "Blau, s.v. …" credit and a site-wide acknowledgment of the 2006 dictionary. My reading is that this is fair, attributed, educational use that sends readers toward your dictionary rather than substituting for it. But I would much rather have your blessing than assume it, and I would gladly adjust the attribution or scope to whatever you would prefer.

### Variant 2 — Offer to license / pay

> I would like to put this on a clear and proper footing. If the Academy considers a licence appropriate for the Blau-derived data in this educational project, I am glad to discuss a fair arrangement. The project is small and non-commercial, so I am hoping for something modest and proportionate — but I would rather pay for clarity and your goodwill than rely on assumptions.

### Variant 3 — Partnership / offer the technology

> In the course of this work I built something I think the Academy may find valuable in its own right: a fully searchable, sense-tagged, Hebrew↔Arabic digital structuring of Blau's dictionary, paired with a "tap any word to read its sense in context" reader for medieval Judeo-Arabic texts. Rather than this living only on my machine, I would be honoured to place it at the Academy's service — to help power an online Blau, or a joint digital edition, under your control and to your standard. I would welcome a conversation about what a partnership could look like.

### Variant 4 — Third-party funding for a joint project

> I believe there is a fundable project here that could serve the Academy without drawing on its budget: a properly licensed, properly edited digital edition of Blau — searchable, linked to real texts, freely usable by students of Judeo-Arabic. I have experience securing grants and fiscal sponsorship for scholarly digital work, and I would be glad to pursue funding for such a project *with* the Academy as the rights holder and partner, if that is of interest.

**How Blau's dictionary is used.** I want to describe this plainly, because honesty matters more to me than any advantage.

- On the **public website**, I do *not* reproduce the dictionary. What I publish is a small set of my own short notes — roughly 150 of them — each observing how Saadia's Arabic diverges from what a Hebrew reader would expect, and each *citing* your dictionary by root (a literal "Blau, s.v. …" label) with a site-wide acknowledgment of the 2006 edition. These notes point readers *toward* Blau; they cannot replace it.
- Separately, on my own computer, I built a **searchable digital structuring of the dictionary** to do this work — an OCR of the print volume, cleaned and organized into a queryable database. This is private research infrastructure; it is not on the website and not served to anyone. I mention it because I would rather you hear it from me, and because I think it could be useful *to you* (see above).

**A few examples**, so you can see the nature of the work — each is an original observation that leans on Blau:

- **אכ'תיאר** (Genesis 4:7) — In God's words to Cain, Saadia reaches for *ikhtiyār* not in its plain classical sense "choice," but in the charged Muʿtazilite sense **"by free will"** — a piece of theology carried in a single word. Blau's dictionary is what lets a reader confirm that post-classical sense.
- **מסלט** (Genesis 1:26–27) — For the human made "in our image," Saadia chooses a word meaning **"ruling, given dominion,"** an anti-anthropomorphic reading: the image of God is *dominion*, not a bodily likeness.
- **גלד** (Genesis 1:6) — For the *raqiaʿ* (firmament), Saadia stretches a root meaning **"hardness, freezing"** — a semantic move classical Arabic does not have, recoverable through the medieval Judeo-Arabic record your dictionary documents.

These are the kinds of moments the site helps a student notice — and they are only legible *because* Blau's dictionary exists.

I have the greatest respect for the dictionary and for the Academy's stewardship of it, and for Professor Blau ז״ל's memory. I would be grateful for a short conversation — by call or in person in Jerusalem — to make sure I am doing right by you, and to explore whether any of this could be useful to the Academy. I am happy to show you exactly what I have built.

With respect and thanks,
